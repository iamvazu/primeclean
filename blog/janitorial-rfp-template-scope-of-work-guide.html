Commercial Janitorial RFP Template & Scope of Work Guide (2026)

✍️ By: Prime Clean Facility Advisory Team
📅 Published: August 2026
⏱️ Read Time: 8 min read
📍 Location: San Francisco Bay Area & East Bay

A flawed Request for Proposal (RFP) is the #1 reason commercial facilities suffer from vendor turnover, scope disputes, and inconsistent cleanings. When vendors bid on vague expectations, property managers end up comparing "apples to oranges." Here is an actionable guide and structured specification matrix to craft an airtight janitorial RFP.

📌 Essential Takeaways for Facility & Property Managers

  • Line-Item Frequencies: Eliminate "as-needed" cleaning clauses; specify exact daily, weekly, and monthly requirements.
  • California Compliance Checks: Require verified DIR registration numbers, $2M+ General Liability, and workers' compensation certs.
  • Mandatory Pre-Bid Walkthrough: Never accept bids from contractors who quote sight-unseen without measuring square footage and floor materials.
  • Digital Proof-of-Performance: Stipulate time-stamped supervisor inspection logs and dedicated emergency dispatch response times.

1. The 6 Core Sections Every Janitorial RFP Must Contain

Whether managing a 15,000 sq ft office in Richmond or a multi-building corporate campus in Walnut Creek, your RFP document should be structured into six clear sections:

  1. Facility Profile & Operating Hours: Total square footage, flooring breakdown (% carpet, % VCT, % polished concrete, % tile), restroom fixture count, building security protocols, and required cleaning service windows (e.g., after 6:00 PM or day porter 8:00 AM – 4:00 PM).
  2. Detailed Scope of Work (SOW): The exact task list separated by building zone and recurrence frequency.
  3. Contractor Qualifications & Compliance: DIR registration (#1001318678 standard), WOSB / SB certifications, minimum 5 years in business, and 3 verifiable client references within the San Francisco Bay Area.
  4. Chemical & Equipment Specifications: EPA List N hospital disinfectants, Green Seal certified detergents, HEPA filtration vacuums, and color-coded microfiber protocols.
  5. Supervision & Communication SLAs: Dedicated on-site supervisor ratio, response time for emergency requests (e.g., under 2 hours), and monthly formal walkthrough reviews.
  6. Transparent Pricing Structure: Fixed monthly janitorial fee, consumable supplies pricing schedule, and hourly rate schedule for ad-hoc specialty services (carpet extraction, window washing, stripping and waxing).

2. Standard Commercial Janitorial Scope of Work Matrix

Use this standardized frequency framework to define responsibilities across each zone of your commercial property:

Facility Zone Daily Tasks (Every Service) Weekly Tasks Monthly / Periodic Tasks
Restrooms Disinfect all toilets, urinals, sinks, counters; polish chrome; restock paper & soap; mop floors with germicidal solution; empty trash. Machine scrub tile & grout lines; descaling acid wash on partitions; clean exhaust vents. High-dusting above 7 ft; deep plumbing scale purge; wall-to-wall disinfection fogging.
Lobbies & Entrances Glass door spot cleaning; vacuum entry mats; disinfect reception desk & kiosk screens; dust mop hard floors. Detail door frames; polish brass/stainless accents; spot-clean upholstered waiting chairs. High glass cleaning; exterior vestibule power wash; entrance mat deep extraction.
Offices & Workstations Empty wastebaskets & recycling; sanitize high-touch handles & light switches; spot vacuum high-traffic carpet paths. Full wall-to-wall HEPA vacuuming; dust horizontal surfaces, file cabinets, and window sills. Baseboard detail; chair caster cleaning; vent diffusers & return air grille dusting.
Breakrooms & Kitchens Disinfect counters, tables, and sink basins; wipe exterior of microwave, fridge, & coffee machines; damp mop floors. Wipe interior of microwaves & toaster trays; detail cabinet face exteriors. Deep refrigerator interior purge & sanitization; hard water scale removal from fixtures.

3. How to Score & Evaluate Vendor Proposals

Avoid picking the lowest bidder solely on price. Lowball quotes frequently lead to missed cleaning days, untrained staff, and mid-contract rate increase demands. We recommend using a 100-point weighted scorecard:

  • 30 Points — Technical Scope & Staffing Plan: Does the bidder provide adequate labor hours for your square footage? (Benchmark: ~3,000–4,000 sq ft per cleaner hour for general offices).
  • 25 Points — Pricing Transparency & Value: Is the bid inclusive of payroll taxes, prevailing wages (if applicable), and equipment? Are supply markups reasonable?
  • 20 Points — Compliance, Insurance & Certifications: Current California DIR registration, active SAM.gov status, $2M+ General Liability, and WOSB / SB status.
  • 15 Points — Quality Control & Tech Systems: Does the vendor have GPS-verified clock-in systems, digital inspection apps, and photo logs?
  • 10 Points — References & Local Reputation: Verifiable case studies from similar property types in Alameda, Contra Costa, or San Francisco counties.
