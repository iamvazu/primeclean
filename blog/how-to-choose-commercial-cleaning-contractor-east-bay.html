Vendor Vetting & Procurement

7 Red Flags When Hiring an East Bay Commercial Cleaning Contractor

✍️ By: Prime Clean Operations Team
📅 Published: August 2026
⏱️ Read Time: 7 min read
📍 Location: East Bay & San Francisco

Commercial cleaning vendor turnover is one of the biggest headaches for facility and property managers in the San Francisco Bay Area. A vendor delivers great service in Month 1, cuts corners by Month 3, and forces you to re-bid by Month 6. Here is how to spot the red flags before signing a contract.

The 7 Warning Signs to Watch For

1. Missing California DIR Registration (Crucial for Public & Prevailing Wage Bids)

In California, any contractor performing janitorial work on public works or government facilities must maintain active registration with the Department of Industrial Relations (DIR). Failure to carry this registration creates severe compliance liabilities for property managers and government agencies. Always request their active DIR registration number.

2. "Subcontracted Franchise" Models with No Direct Supervision

Many national janitorial brands sell cleaning routes to independent franchisees with little quality control. When quality drops, the corporate office blames the franchisee, and the franchisee blames corporate. Work with a direct contractor who employs and directly manages their own background-checked supervisors.

3. Vague "Lump-Sum" Bids Without Itemized Frequencies

A reputable commercial cleaner provides a line-by-line breakdown of daily, weekly, and monthly tasks (e.g., daily high-touch disinfection, weekly low-dusting, monthly vent sanitizing). Vague scopes lead to disputes over what was included.

4. Inadequate General Liability & Workers' Comp Coverage

Commercial facilities must demand a minimum of $2,000,000 in General Liability insurance, comprehensive bonding, and valid Workers' Compensation certificates naming your property as additionally insured.

5. No Digital Proof of Performance or Service Logs

Assurances are cheap; documentation is accountable. Modern contractors use digital time-stamped supervisor logs and structured quality-audit walkthroughs so you have verifiable audit records of every visit.

6. No Dedicated Local Point of Contact

If you have an urgent spill, biohazard, or inspection issue, dialing a 1-800 call center wastes valuable hours. Demand a named local operations supervisor with a direct cell number.

7. Bids That Are Drastically Below Market Benchmark

If three contractors bid $2,000/mo and one bids $900/mo, that low bidder is likely cutting corners on wage laws, skipping supervisor oversight, or using diluted chemicals. You will end up replacing them within 90 days.

📋 Janitorial Vendor Vetting Checklist

  • ✅ Proof of California Small Business (SB) or WOSB Certification
  • ✅ Current Certificate of Insurance ($2M+ General Liability + Workers' Comp)
  • ✅ Active DIR Registration & SAM.gov Unique Entity Identifier (UEI)
  • ✅ Color-coded microfiber cross-contamination protocol
  • ✅ Documented supervisor inspection schedule
