## 2. Competitive Displacement Strategy (businesses with an existing vendor)

The reality: most commercial cleaning contracts aren't won by being cheaper — they're won by finding the moment a facility manager is already frustrated and being the obvious, low-risk alternative right when it happens.

**A. Target the pain, not the price**

Most businesses don't switch janitorial vendors because of price. They switch because of:
- Inconsistent quality (missed nights, corners not vacuumed, trash not emptied)
- Poor communication (can't reach anyone when something's wrong)
- High turnover on the cleaning crew (different people every week, no accountability)
- No real point of contact — just a call center or app

The outreach and sales conversation should lead with these pain points, not with "we're cheaper." Ask directly: "How's your current cleaning company doing? Ever had a night where something got missed and you couldn't get a straight answer on why?"

**B. The "Contract Audit" offer**

Instead of asking a business to switch cold, offer a **free, no-obligation Cleaning Contract Audit**: Jackie walks the facility, reviews what they're currently paying and what's actually being delivered, and provides a written comparison — scope, frequency, and price — side by side with what Prime Clean would offer. This is low-commitment framing ("just get a second opinion") that gets a foot in the door even when someone isn't actively shopping.

**C. Time it around contract renewal windows**

Most janitorial contracts are 12-month terms, often with 30-60 day auto-renewal notice clauses. Two tactics:
- In the outreach email/call, simply ask: "When does your current contract come up for renewal?" and set a follow-up reminder for 60 days before that date — this is when facility managers are actually evaluating.
- For government/property-management targets specifically, RFP and bid renewal cycles are often public or semi-predictable (check municipal procurement calendars) — time outreach 60-90 days ahead of those.

**D. De-risk the switch**

The biggest reason businesses stay with a mediocre vendor is switching friction, not loyalty. Address this directly:
- Offer a 30-day satisfaction guarantee or a month-to-month start (no long-term lock-in) so trying Prime Clean feels reversible
- Offer to handle the transition logistics (key/badge handoff, supply closet setup, schedule coordination) so it's zero extra work for the facility manager
- Provide 2-3 references they can call before signing — social proof reduces perceived risk more than a lower price does

**E. Bundle the "why us" into something tangible**

Rather than a generic pitch, send a one-page comparison sheet: "What to look for in a janitorial contract" — an educational piece (not overtly salesy) that happens to list DIR registration, certified/insured status, dedicated account contact, documented scope of work, etc. — the exact things Prime Clean has and many small unlicensed operators don't. This positions Prime Clean as the informed, safer choice without directly bad-mouthing the competitor.

## 3. Novel Ideas to Generate an Inbound Call for a Walkthrough

Ranked roughly by effort-to-impact ratio:

**1. The "Free Facility Walkthrough & Cleanliness Score" offer**
Instead of asking businesses to "get a quote" (which feels transactional and salesy), offer a **free walkthrough that ends in a written Cleanliness & Compliance Score** — covering things like restroom sanitation, high-touch surface frequency, floor care, and OSHA-relevant items. This reframes the ask from "let us sell you something" to "get a free professional assessment," which has a much lower barrier for a facility manager to say yes to, and it naturally leads into a quote afterward.

**2. Direct mail to the physical facility, addressed to the Facilities Manager by title**
Email gets ignored; a well-designed, short postcard mailed directly to "Facilities Manager" or "Office Manager" at the business address stands out simply because almost no one does it anymore. Include a QR code straight to a booking page for the free walkthrough. Works especially well for property management companies and medical offices, where the same generic front-desk email address is otherwise your only way in.

**3. LinkedIn + local Chamber of Commerce visibility**
Jackie personally connects with facility managers, office managers, and practice administrators in the 22 target cities on LinkedIn, and posts short before/after content, WOSB/DIR credential highlights, and local project shoutouts (with permission). Join the Richmond, Contra Costa, and relevant local Chambers of Commerce — WOSB/small-business certified vendors often get preferential visibility in chamber vendor directories and B2B mixers, which is a warm-intro channel most competitors skip.

**4. Vertical-specific "trigger event" outreach**
Watch for public signals that a business is about to need a new cleaning vendor and reach out right at that moment:
- New commercial leases / new office openings (permit filings, local business journal announcements)
- Businesses that just passed a health inspection with cleanliness citations (public records for medical/food-adjacent facilities)
- New government facility openings or renovations (public bid/procurement notices)
- A competitor's negative Google/Yelp review mentioning cleaning quality at a nearby business — an opening to reach out to that business's neighbors in the same building/complex

**5. "Referral bonus" for existing clients and even for facility managers who say no**
Offer existing satisfied clients a referral incentive (discount on next month, or a straightforward cash/gift-card referral fee) for introducing Prime Clean to another facility manager. Also — counterintuitively — when a prospect declines because they're happy with their current vendor, ask them directly if they know another business that might not be. Facility managers know each other regionally; this costs nothing and often produces the best leads.

**6. Sponsor or show up at local business events, not with a booth pitch but with a giveaway**
A free "Deep Clean Giveaway" raffle at a local Chamber of Commerce mixer or small-business expo — a full deep clean for one winning business — gets Prime Clean in front of dozens of facility decision-makers at once, generates an email list of every entrant, and gives Jackie a natural reason to follow up with everyone who entered but didn't win ("since you didn't win the giveaway, want a free walkthrough and quote instead?").

**7. Google Business Profile + review velocity focused on the 22 target cities**
Make sure the Google Business Profile lists all 22 service-area cities and actively request reviews after every completed job, specifically asking happy clients to mention the *industry/vertical* they're in (e.g., "great for our medical office"). Facility managers searching "janitorial services near me" or "office cleaning [city]" convert heavily off review count and recency — this is a compounding, low-cost channel that also directly supports the pSEO location pages already built into the site.

## 4. Is a Free First Clean Smart?

**Short answer: use it selectively, not as a blanket offer.**

**Why it can work:**
- Removes almost all risk from trying a new vendor — the facility manager can judge quality firsthand rather than trusting a sales pitch
- Strong for facilities that are actively dissatisfied with their current vendor and just need a low-risk way to compare
- Good as a *close* tactic after the walkthrough ("let us prove it — first clean's on us"), rather than a blanket upfront marketing offer

**Why it can backfire if used broadly:**
- Attracts price-shoppers and one-time freebie-seekers rather than serious facility managers looking to actually switch vendors
- Devalues the service in the prospect's mind before they've even seen the quote — makes the first real invoice feel like a price increase
- Costs real labor and supplies with no guaranteed conversion, and doesn't scale well across a large outreach campaign

**Recommended approach — a hybrid:**
Don't lead with "free first clean" in the cold outreach email or in general marketing. Instead:
1. Lead with the **free walkthrough + cleanliness assessment** (item #1 above) — costs Jackie time, not cleaning labor, and it's the actual sales conversation.
2. During or right after the walkthrough, if the prospect is on the fence, offer the free first clean as a **closing tool**, not an opening one: "Let us clean it once at no charge so you can see the difference before you commit to anything." This converts far better because it's offered to someone who's already engaged, not blasted to everyone.
3. Alternatively, offer a **discounted first month (e.g., 50% off)** instead of fully free — this still lowers risk substantially, but the prospect has skin in the game, which correlates with a real intent to switch rather than a freebie grab.

## 5. Other Tactics to Win the Contract After a Walkthrough

1. **Send the written proposal within 24 hours of the walkthrough.** Facility managers who wait a week for a quote often move on or forget urgency. Speed signals professionalism and organizational competence — which is exactly what you're selling.

2. **Make the proposal a comparison, not just a quote.** Lay out current vendor (if known) vs. Prime Clean side by side: scope, frequency, certifications, insurance, single point of contact. This does the "why switch" thinking for them.

3. **Offer a short trial period instead of demanding a 12-month contract upfront.** A 60- or 90-day trial with an easy opt-out removes the biggest objection to signing (fear of being locked into another bad vendor).

4. **Provide 2-3 references proactively, not just on request.** Include names/contacts of similar-vertical clients (e.g., another medical office, another property management client) directly in the proposal.

5. **Include the DIR registration, WOSB, and CA Small Business certification numbers directly on the proposal document.** For any business that has government contracts, supplier-diversity goals, or vendor compliance requirements, this can be a genuine differentiator, not just a badge — it may let them meet internal diversity-spend targets.

6. **Follow up with a personal note or call within 48 hours of sending the proposal**, not just an email drip. A short phone call ("just wanted to see if you had any questions on the proposal") closes more of these than automated follow-up sequences.

7. **For property management and multi-site prospects specifically**, propose a small pilot site first (one building) rather than asking for the whole portfolio at once — makes the decision smaller and easier to say yes to, and sets up a natural expansion conversation once they see the results.
